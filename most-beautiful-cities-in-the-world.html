The 10 Most Beautiful Cities in the World, According to the People Who Live There

tripspace.global Editorial · Cape Town

Beauty is subjective — but ask enough people the same question, in enough cities, and a real pattern emerges. That's exactly what Time Out did in 2026: they asked 24,000 residents across 118 cities worldwide one simple question — would you describe your city as "beautiful"? The answers produced the most credible, locally-sourced beauty ranking in recent memory, and the results might surprise you.

This isn't a list built from a single travel writer's opinion or a magazine's editorial pick. It's aggregated from the people who wake up in these cities every day — the ones with no reason to oversell their hometown, and every reason to be honest about it. Time Out's Travel Editor Grace Beard led the 2026 survey, published on 26 June 2026, ranking the top 20 cities by the percentage of locals who called their own city beautiful. Here's the top 10.

1. Cape Town, South Africa — 86%

Photo: Pixabay

86% of Capetonians — the highest score of any city surveyed — call their own city beautiful, and it's not hard to see why. Cape Town climbs the slopes between the flat-topped Table Mountain and the Atlantic coastline, framed by the Cape Floral Kingdom's botanical diversity and the dramatic cliffs of the Twelve Apostles. Time Out's Cape Town city editor, Selene Brophy, points to the city's award-winning airport, its character-filled neighbourhoods, and the genuine warmth of its people — from the flower sellers in the city centre to the fishermen at Kalk Bay — as reasons it consistently tops lists like this one.

2. Edinburgh, Scotland — 84%

Photo: Pixabay

Edinburgh's beauty comes from genuine architectural contrast — the New Town's elegant Georgian crescents sitting a short walk from the Old Town's tight medieval closes and Gothic spires. Locals rank it among the most walkable cities on the planet, and the view from Calton Hill, overlooking Edinburgh Castle and the Old Town skyline, is considered one of the finest urban panoramas in Europe.

3. Sydney, Australia — 78%

Photo: Pixabay

Few skylines are as instantly recognisable as Sydney's — the Opera House's sail-like shells set against the steel arch of the Harbour Bridge. Add golden sandstone streets, glassy modern towers, and a ring of genuinely excellent beaches within the city limits, and it's easy to understand why 78% of Sydneysiders call their city beautiful without hesitation.

4. Chicago, United States — 77%

Photo: Pixabay

Chicago's reputation is built on its skyline — but the survey found something less expected: it's genuinely one of the greenest big cities on the planet, with hundreds of parks threaded between its skyscrapers. Combined with sweeping Lake Michigan views and the winding Chicago River, it edges out every other American city on the list, the highest-ranked city in the United States in Time Out's beauty survey.

5. Lisbon, Portugal — 74%

Photo: Pixabay

Lisbon's light is genuinely different — a coastal glow that makes everything feel gently gilded. Yellow trams cruise down steep, pastel-lined streets, hand-painted azulejo tiles cover entire building façades, and the city's seven hills mean nearly every walk ends in a viewpoint. 74% of locals agree it's beautiful, and once you've seen it, it's hard to disagree.

6. Paris, France — 68%

Photo: Pixabay

No list like this would be credible without Paris. The French capital famously resisted the skyscraper boom other global cities embraced, staying instead a city of grand boulevards, wrought-iron balconies, and Haussmannian stone façades. Add the Seine, the city's parks, and its instantly recognisable tower, and Paris earns its place — even if, at 68%, its own residents are more measured about it than visitors tend to be.

7. Stockholm, Sweden — 68%

Photo: Pixabay

Stockholm is, quite literally, an archipelago — a city built across 14 island neighbourhoods where Lake Mälaren meets the Baltic Sea, connected by more than 50 bridges. From the narrow medieval lanes of Gamla Stan (the Old Town) to the bohemian hilltop streets of Södermalm, the city's relationship with water gives it a genuinely distinct visual identity few capitals can match.

8. Porto, Portugal — 67%

Photo: Pixabay

Porto is a city that cascades — red-roofed buildings tumbling down toward the Douro River, best appreciated from the hilltop Jardim do Morro across the water. Its architecture is a genuine patchwork: the blue-and-white tiled interior of São Bento Station, weathered riverside houses, and Art Deco cinema façades all sitting within a few real minutes of each other.

9. Medellín, Colombia — 66%

Photo: Pixabay

Medellín sits inside the Aburrá Valley, a natural river basin that keeps lush, green mountainside visible from almost anywhere in the city. Its architecture blends colonial-era churches with genuinely nature-first structures like the Orquideorama — a honeycomb-shaped wooden canopy inside the city's botanical garden. It's the only South American city to make the global top 10.

10. Riga, Latvia — 65%

Photo: Pixabay

Riga rounds out the list as one of the world's great centres for Art Nouveau architecture, concentrated along the UNESCO-listed Alberta Street — gargoyles, sphinxes, and elaborate floral motifs carved directly into building façades. Beyond that, a classic cobbled Old Town and a wealth of medieval buildings give Riga a genuinely layered, walkable charm.

What this means if you're actually planning a trip

A survey like this is more than a curiosity — it's a genuinely useful signal for where real, on-the-ground pride in a place translates into an experience worth having as a visitor. Every city on this list rewards simply walking around without much of an agenda, which is often the surest sign a place is worth the trip.
