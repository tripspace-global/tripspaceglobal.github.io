Damaged a rental car in Cape Town?
What to do before you pay the excess.

Travel Tips Jarrit Hosking November 2026 6 min read

A scraped bumper in a tight Camps Bay car park. A door ding at the Waterfront. A stone-chipped bonnet on the road to Hout Bay. It happens to a lot of visitors, and the first thing most people do is panic about the bill. Slow down. A few steps taken in the right order can save you real money and a lot of stress.

This guide comes from the workshop floor. International Panel Shop in Cape Town has repaired a great many rental cars over the years, and the same questions come up every time. Here is what we tell people.

1. Report it to the rental company first

Before anything else, tell the rental company. Most agreements require you to report damage promptly, and some set a time limit. Reporting first also protects you: it puts on record that you were upfront about it.

While you do, take clear photos from every angle, wide shots that show the whole car and the location, and close-ups of the damage. If it was a collision with another vehicle or a person's property, get a police report and the other driver's details.

2. Read your rental agreement

Find three things in your agreement: the excess (the amount you pay toward any damage), whether you bought a damage waiver that reduces it, and what the agreement says about who may repair the car. Many travellers never read this section until something goes wrong.

3. Ask in writing whether you may use your own repairer

Plenty of rental companies are relaxed and say you can take the car where you like. Others want it repaired through their own network. Both are normal, and you only find out which applies to you by asking.

"Don't assume. Ask, and get the answer in writing."

A simple email or message is enough: "I have damaged the car. May I get a quote from a repairer of my choice, and will you accept the repair under the agreement?" Keep their reply. If they say no, or want their own repairer, follow their instructions. Repairing a rental car without permission can cause you trouble later.

4. Get a clear quote before you decide

Prices depend on the car, the panel, the paint and whether anything is hidden under the surface. Nobody can tell you an honest final price without seeing the car, but you can get a good idea quickly. Send clear photos to a panel shop and ask for an estimate. A reputable shop will tell you straight what is a quick fix and what needs a closer look.

You can also get a head start inside TripSpace. TripGuard is our free AI rental car damage assessment tool: upload your photos and get an estimate you can use as a reference when talking to the rental company or a repairer.

5. Compare the repair cost with your excess

Sometimes the repair costs less than the excess. Sometimes it costs more. Once the rental company has told you what it will accept, put both numbers side by side. Knowing the figures before you decide means you are choosing with the facts, not guessing.

Quick checklist

  • Report the damage to the rental company and take photos from every angle
  • Get a police report if another vehicle or person was involved
  • Check your excess, any damage waiver and the repair clause
  • Ask in writing whether you may use your own repairer
  • Get a written quote and compare it with the excess
  • Keep every document: permission, quote, invoice and photos

7. Keep the paperwork

Whatever you decide, keep the rental company's written permission, the quote and invoice, your before-and-after photos and any police report. If anyone questions the repair later, this folder is your proof.

Damage on holiday is annoying, but it does not have to ruin the trip. Report it, ask the right questions, compare the figures and keep your records. Do that and you are already ahead of most people in the same spot.
